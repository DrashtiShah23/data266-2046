Experiment 1: Baseline DDPM Training

Dataset: MNIST

Beta schedule: linear from 0.0001 to 0.02

Optimizer: Adam
Learning rate: 0.001
Loss: MSE
Trainable parameters: 82,945
Device: NVIDIA Tesla T4

Results:
Initial loss = 0.156066
Final loss = 0.062526
Best loss = 0.062490 at epoch 48
Loss reduction = 59.94%
Total training time = 635.28 seconds

Evaluation:
The baseline model achieved a low noise prediction loss, but 100 step DDPM sampling produced overlapping and poorly structured digit like strokes. The training objective was therefore not sufficient by itself to establish generation quality.

Decision:
Increase the effective receptive field of the noise prediction network using downsampling, upsampling, and skip connections.

Use the improved model for the final DDPM and DDIM comparison.

Experiment 4: DDIM Sampling

DDIM 50 steps:
Sampling time = 0.1354 seconds
Speedup over DDPM = 1.75x
Time reduction = 42.77%

DDIM 20 steps:
Sampling time = 0.0499 seconds
Speedup over DDPM = 4.74x
Time reduction = 78.89%

Result:
Reducing the number of DDIM sampling steps substantially improved generation speed. The 50 step configuration retained more recognizable structure than the 20 step configuration, while the 20 step configuration provided the largest speed improvement.


Final Observation

The experiment demonstrates that noise prediction loss alone does not fully represent generative quality. The improved architecture produced better global structure, while DDIM demonstrated the expected computational advantage of generating samples using fewer reverse diffusion steps.
